George Brown College

George Brown College is a public college located in Toronto,
Ontario, Canada. It offers programs in technology, business,
health sciences, design, and other fields.

The college focuses on practical education, industry connections,
and career preparation. Students gain knowledge and skills that
help them prepare for professional careers.

The college was established during the formation of Ontario's community college system in 1967. 
Colleges of Applied Arts and Technology were established on May 21, 1965. 
The college is named after George Brown, who was an important 19th-century politician and newspaper
 publisher (he founded the Toronto Globe, forerunner to The Globe and Mail) and was one 
 of the Fathers of Confederation.

 The college's predecessor, the Provincial Institute of Trades (PIT), was founded in 1951 to offer apprentice 
 training on behalf of the provincial Department of Labour.In 1952, the PIT began operation at 21 Nassau Street
  in Toronto's Kensington Market and, after expanding with the construction of two additional buildings on the 
  site, was offering programs in lathing and structural steel, barbering, diesel mechanics, jewellery arts,
   watchmaking and welding by 1961. In 1962 the province opened the Provincial Institute of Trades and Occupations 
   (PITO), a sister training institute, at 555 Davenport Road near Casa Loma.
